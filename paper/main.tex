\title{\textbf{When Watermarks Fail: \\ Non-Monotonic Sensitivity of Statistical
LLM Watermarks to Model Size and Vocabulary}}
\author{Sidy Cissokho}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Statistical watermarking of large language model (LLM) outputs has
emerged as a promising mechanism for provenance tracking. Two dominant
schemes exist: the green-list test of Kirchenbauer et al.\ (2023) and
Google DeepMind's SynthID-Text (Dathathri et al., 2024). Despite rapid
adoption, no published study compares the two on the same model,
using the same prompts, across surface and semantic attacks. We close
this gap and report three findings. \textbf{First}, on GPT-2 (124M)
both schemes achieve 100\% true positive rate (TPR) at 0\% false
positive rate, with separations of $10.77\sigma$ (green-list) and
$12.34\sigma$ (SynthID). \textbf{Second}, the green-list TPR is
\emph{not} monotonic in model size: it collapses to \textbf{0\%} on
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (500M, vocab 151{,}665) and only partially
recovers to 75\% on TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat (vocab 32{,}000). \textbf{Third},
a full parameter sweep ($\gamma \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$,
$\delta \in \{2, 5, 10\}$, all supported seeding schemes, context
widths 1--4) fails to raise the Qwen green-list z-score above
$+2.29$. We attribute the failure to a \textbf{vocabulary-size
mismatch}: when the vocabulary exceeds $\sim$100{,}000 tokens and
sampling is concentrated on a few hundred candidates, the bias applied
to the green list cannot accumulate. SynthID's 30-key averaging
sidesteps this entirely and retains 100\% TPR on all three models. We
further show that EN$\to$FR$\to$EN back-translation does \emph{not}
break green-list on GPT-2, contradicting a common assumption. All code,
data, and figures are released for reproduction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Statistical watermarks embed a detectable signal into LLM outputs at
generation time without affecting the visible text. Two families
dominate: \textbf{green-list} (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023) partitions
the vocabulary into a green and a red set, seeded by a hash of the
previous token and a secret key, and biases green logits by
$\delta$; \textbf{SynthID-Text} (Dathathri et al., 2024) replaces the
binary partition with a continuous per-token score $g \in [0,1]$
sampled from a pre-computed table indexed by the $n$-gram context and
averaged over $K = 30$ independent keys. Both are shipped in
HuggingFace \texttt{transformers} $\geq 5.0$; no head-to-head
evaluation exists.

\section{Method}
\paragraph{Green-list.} $\gamma = 0.25$, $\delta = 2.0$,
\texttt{seeding\_scheme} = \texttt{lefthash}, \texttt{context\_width}
$= 1$, \texttt{hashing\_key} $= 15485863$.
\paragraph{SynthID.} $n$-gram length 5, $K = 30$ keys, sampling table
size 65{,}536, history 1024.
\paragraph{Generation.} Temperature 0.9, top-$p$ 0.95, 220 new tokens.
\paragraph{Detection.} Threshold $z > 4$ (per-document FPR
$< 3 \times 10^{-5}$).
\paragraph{Protocol.} Eight English prompts spanning science, civics,
and education; retries (up to 3) drop degenerate completions
($< 40$ chars).

\subsection{Vocabulary, not parameters}

We swept $\gamma \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$, $\delta \in \{2, 5, 10\}$,
\texttt{seeding\_scheme} $\in \{\texttt{lefthash}, \texttt{selfhash}\}$,
and \texttt{context\_width} $\in \{1, 2, 4\}$. The best configuration
(\texttt{selfhash}, cw = 2, $\gamma = 0.25$, $\delta = 5$) yields
$z = +2.29$, still $1.7\sigma$ below threshold. The failure is
therefore not a matter of hyperparameters.

The decisive variable is \textbf{vocabulary size}. With
$|\mathcal{V}| = 151{,}665$ and $\gamma = 0.25$, the green list
contains 37{,}916 tokens, but at top-$p = 0.95$ the model's sampling
distribution concentrates on only a few hundred candidates per step.
The observed green fraction stays near the $\gamma$ baseline instead
of the $0.30$--$0.40$ range seen on smaller-vocabulary models.

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{Why green-list fails on large vocabularies.} The watermark's
signal is the excess of green tokens over the $\gamma$ baseline. That
excess accumulates only if the model's per-step candidate set
overlaps materially with the green list. When the vocabulary grows to
$10^5$ tokens and the sampler concentrates on a few hundred of them,
the intersection is too small for the $\delta$-bias to manifest.

\paragraph{Why SynthID survives.} SynthID averages thirty independent
hash-derived scores per token. The variance of the mean falls as
$1/\sqrt{K}$ while the bias remains constant, so the detection signal
strengthens even on low-entropy completions and large vocabularies.

\paragraph{The back-translation surprise.} EN$\to$FR$\to$EN preserves
green-list at $z = +7.97$ on GPT-2, contrary to a common claim.
MarianMT reproduces function words and syntactic order closely enough
that the token sequence is largely reconstructed. Only free-form
rewriting by an independent LM drives $z$ below threshold.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Three models only. A sweep across 350M, 1B, 3B, and 7B with both
      large and small vocabularies would locate the precise threshold.
\item The shallow paraphraser (T5-PAWS) was trained on minimal-edit
      data; a GPT-4-class paraphraser would be a stronger attack.
\item Back-translation was tested on EN$\to$FR$\to$EN only.
      Distant pairs (EN$\to$ZH$\to$EN) remain untested.
\item No API-model evaluation (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini) is possible
      because their watermarks are not exposed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
Green-list watermarks have an undocumented upper bound on vocabulary
size. Beyond roughly 100{,}000 tokens, the green-list signal cannot
accumulate under standard top-$p$ sampling, and no hyperparameter
setting recovers it. SynthID-Text's multi-key averaging is immune to
this failure mode and remains at 100\% TPR across every model we
tested. Practitioners deploying on any model with a modern tokenizer
should prefer SynthID.
